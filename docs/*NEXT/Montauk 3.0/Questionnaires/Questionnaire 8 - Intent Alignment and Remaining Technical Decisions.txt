itself - without letting the search set its own passing grade or silently

B1. (was Q7 #13) Is the false-Gold promise per year, or for all time?

Plain version. We want to keep the odds low that a worthless strategy ever
sneaks onto the board by luck. The plan currently uses "about 1% chance per
year." But the machine runs forever, and a steady 1% per year is roughly a 10%
chance over ten years. That is a different promise.

Simple example. Two ways to run it:
  - Per-year budget: every year gets a fresh 1% allowance. Simple and stable, but
    the risk quietly accumulates over the appliance's life.
  - Lifetime budget: 1% total, ever. Much stricter - but because the budget is
    spent down every time we certify anything, the bar keeps rising and after
    enough years almost nothing can pass.

Question. Which promise do you want Montauk to make?

Suggested answer. Use a per-year control, and make the accumulated multi-year
risk visible rather than hidden - the digest states "at the current setting, the
chance that at least one false Gold has ever been admitted is now about X%." Have
Phase 1 show you annual, 5-year, 10-year, and lifetime numbers before you fix the
level.

Reasoning. A true lifetime budget on a system meant to run indefinitely
eventually strangles the board - it would stop finding anything, which is not what
you want either. Disclosure gives you the honest number without that trap. Note
also that the "FDR" method the research package proposed does not actually deliver
an any-false-Gold guarantee; if you want that specific promise, it needs a
different method, which is part of what Phase 1 must compare.
