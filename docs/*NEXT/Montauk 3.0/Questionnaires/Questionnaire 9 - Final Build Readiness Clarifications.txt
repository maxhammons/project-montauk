1. WHAT DOES VALIDATION SCORE MEAN?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 A4 was left blank. Earlier answers said the score
should not show a percentage unless that percentage has been proved trustworthy.
The plan still needs a clear statement of what the number means before the score
and leaderboard are built.

Plain version. A strategy might show:

  Validation Score: 78

That should not imply "78% chance of future success." Montauk does not yet have
enough frozen forward outcomes to support that interpretation.

Question. Should Validation Score initially mean the strength and consistency of
the evidence supporting a strategy, without a percent sign or probability claim?

Suggested answer. Yes. Validation Score measures how strongly and consistently
the strategy passed the evidence checks across time, parameter neighborhoods,
correctness tests, and relevant stresses. Show no percent sign. Show the two or
three main reasons the strategy lost points. Montauk may use probability or
"confidence" language only after enough frozen forward results demonstrate that
the score is calibrated, followed by an owner-approved contract change.

Reasoning. This gives the number a useful and honest meaning without creating a
false impression of certainty.

2. HOW SHOULD MONTAUK CHOOSE ITS LONG-RUN FALSE-GOLD PROTECTION?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 B1 was not answered because the underlying risk was not
clear. A candidate must pass both the economic backtest and the anti-overfitting
suite, but those layers are tests rather than guarantees. They use the same
limited TECL history and can share blind spots.

Simple example. If Montauk searches an enormous number of worthless strategies,
one can look unusually good by luck. The same lucky historical shape can
sometimes also pass an anti-overfitting test. Requiring both layers makes this
less likely, but it does not make the probability zero. The entire conveyor must
therefore be tested on known-worthless and planted-good control worlds.

Two possible long-run policies are:

  - Annual control: keep the chance low each year, while disclosing that the
    chance of ever admitting at least one false Gold grows over many years.
  - Lifetime control: keep one finite risk budget for the appliance's whole
    life, which is stricter but can make new Gold increasingly difficult to earn.

Question. Should Phase 1 measure both policies and bring Max the real tradeoff
before any final false-Gold contract is enabled?

Suggested answer. Yes. Do not choose annual versus lifetime or a numerical level
blindly now. Phase 1 must test the complete pipeline on known controls and show,
in plain language:

  - how often a worthless strategy becomes Gold;
  - how often a genuinely useful planted strategy is rejected;
  - annual, five-year, ten-year, and lifetime interpretations;
  - how the bar changes as Montauk searches more strategies; and
  - the assumptions and limitations behind each result.

Max approves the final operating policy before Gold certification is enabled.
Until then, no method may be described as guaranteeing that a false Gold can
never occur.

Reasoning. This preserves all the backtesting and anti-overfitting layers while
measuring whether the combined system actually delivers the protection they are
supposed to provide.

3. WHAT HAPPENS TO A SEALED HISTORICAL HOLDOUT AFTER IT IS USED?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 C2 asked whether Montauk should maintain a permanently
sealed historical block or treat it as one-time evidence. The answer stated the
goal of maximum robustness but did not choose between the two.

Simple example. Montauk hides 2019-2021 from Strategy A. Strategy A fails that
exam, and the agent learns only the word "failed." Strategy B is then designed
in response. Strategy B has indirectly learned from 2019-2021 even if it never
read those prices. After repeated attempts, the block is no longer untouched.

Question. Should a historical holdout be marked spent as soon as any result from
it is revealed?

Suggested answer. Yes. A sealed historical block supplies one-time evidence.
After any pass/fail result, score, rank, or metric is revealed, label it
spent/reused and include the reveal in Montauk's lifetime search accounting.
Only market bars arriving after an exact strategy was frozen may be called
genuinely untouched forward evidence for that strategy.

Reasoning. A permanent historical lockbox in a lifelong feedback loop creates
false comfort. Spent-on-reveal accounting and the growing live-forward record are
the more honest safeguards.

4. WHEN DOES MONTAUK 3.0 REPLACE MONTAUK 2.0 AS THE SIGNAL AUTHORITY?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 C5 used the word "cutover," which was understandably
read as switching from one Active strategy to another. Here, cutover means the
different event where Montauk 3.0 replaces Montauk 2.0 as the system that
produces the trusted daily signal.

Simple example. Montauk 3.0 is running correctly in shadow, but no strategy has
yet earned activation-eligible 3.0 Gold. If 2.0 is retired at that point, there
is no system producing a certified signal.

Question. Should Montauk 2.0 continue producing the clearly labelled legacy
signal until a 3.0 strategy is ready and Max explicitly switches authority?

Suggested answer. Yes. Run 3.0 in shadow while 2.0 remains the legacy signal
source. Block authority cutover until:

  1. at least one strategy has earned 3.0 Gold;
  2. it has completed the required cooling/forward-evidence period;
  3. it is activation-eligible;
  4. the shadow and recovery checks have passed; and
  5. Max approves that exact strategy as Active in the same action that retires
     the 2.0 signal authority.

Until then, the interface must clearly distinguish "legacy 2.0 signal" from
"3.0 shadow recommendation." This is separate from later switches between two
3.0 Gold strategies.

Reasoning. This prevents a gap with no signal while preserving the rule that no
2.0 strategy is grandfathered into 3.0 Gold.

5. EXACTLY HOW MAY SYNTHETIC PRE-2009 HISTORY AFFECT GOLD?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 A5 said to treat the reconstruction as essentially real
data with less weight, while also recognizing that it is not official TECL and
that weak performance there is not a dealbreaker. Those statements express a
clear practical instinct, but the implementation needs one exact authority
boundary.

Plain version. The historical technology-market movements are real. The TECL
price path is reconstructed rather than observed, because TECL did not yet
exist. Calling the series "observed TECL" would therefore be inaccurate even if
the reconstruction proves very good.

Question. Is the following the intended boundary?

Suggested answer. Use the synthetic history after the overlap/reconstruction
study validates and versions it. It may:

  - appear in every relevant diagnostic report;
  - test behavior in older markets and named events;
  - contribute a limited, empirically calibrated amount to Validation Score and
    ranking; and
  - help Max compare already-Gold strategies.

It may not:

  - substitute for passing either required real-TECL economic gate;
  - rescue a strategy that fails real-data Gold requirements;
  - independently deny Gold merely because older synthetic performance was
    weak; or
  - be labelled observed or official TECL.

Any exact weight is proposed from the time-separated overlap study and approved
as part of the signed validation contract; it is not chosen to help favored
strategies.

Reasoning. This uses the older information rather than discarding it, while
honoring the asterisk and the instruction that failure there is not a
dealbreaker.

6. HOW DOES A PASSWORD SEAL THE PROTECTED CORE?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 A6 selected a password. A password can be the human
interaction, but a password check implemented inside writable Montauk code is
not a security boundary: an agent with permission to rewrite that code could
rewrite the check.

Plain version. Max should be able to experience the release process as "I enter
my password and seal this version." Underneath that interaction, the system still
needs permissions and cryptographic verification that the resident agent cannot
bypass.

Question. Is this password-based release ceremony acceptable?

Suggested answer. Yes:

  1. The resident agent runs as an unprivileged account that cannot write the
     protected core.
  2. Max enters the password only into a local, separate privileged maintenance
     process—never Slack, another chat channel, or an AI prompt.
  3. The password unlocks an encrypted signing credential or equivalent
     privileged promotion mechanism.
  4. That process signs and installs one exact reviewed release read-only.
  5. Startup, Gold certification, and signal generation verify the signed
     manifest and fail closed if anything differs.
  6. The agent cannot read the password, signing credential, or a decrypted
     maintenance session.

Reasoning. Max gets the password-controlled sealing workflow he requested, while
the number-one no-pipeline-edits rule remains mechanically enforceable. A plain
password prompt inside the application, Git history, or later rollback is not
sufficient by itself.

7. DOES A MATERIAL TECL PRODUCT CHANGE STOP TRUSTED SIGNALS?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 7 item 11 asked whether a material TECL change should
only alert Max or should also suspend trusted signals and stale Gold. The answer
was "Alert human." The active plan interpreted that more strongly: alert,
suspend signals, stale certificates, and recertify. That interpretation was
never explicitly confirmed.

Ordinary splits and distributions are not the issue; they follow the tested
corporate-action path.

Simple example. Direxion changes TECL's benchmark or leverage objective. Every
Gold certificate was earned for the old product contract. Continuing to issue a
trusted signal would treat the changed product as if it were the same thing that
Montauk tested.

Question. After a benchmark change, leverage-objective change, ticker/CUSIP
discontinuity, closure, prolonged halt, or similarly material prospectus event,
should Montauk only alert Max, or should it also suspend trusted signals?

Suggested answer. Immediately alert Max. Once the change becomes effective,
suspend new trusted signals, mark every affected Gold certificate stale, and
require an owner-approved contract update and full recertification. Never
automatically substitute another ETF. Max remains responsible for any brokerage
position decision during the suspension.

Reasoning. A material product change alters what was certified. Suspending does
not make a trade or choose a replacement; it prevents Montauk from describing an
untested instruction as trusted.

8. ON WHAT DATE DOES THE $1,000 STRATEGY-VERSUS-B&H COMPARISON BEGIN?
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 8 A3 defined the desired comparison as investing $1,000
in the strategy versus $1,000 in TECL B&H "from when the strategy started
trading." That could mean either the first date the strategy was capable of
making a decision or its first actual risk-on trade. Those produce materially
different results.

Simple example. A strategy has all its inputs in 2015 but remains risk-off until
after a large TECL decline in 2018. If the comparison starts only at the first
2018 purchase, the strategy receives no credit or penalty for its 2015-2018 cash
decision and avoids the B&H decline by redefining the start date after seeing
the path.

Question. Should the comparison begin on the first date the strategy was
eligible to make a causal decision, rather than its first actual TECL purchase?

Suggested answer. Yes. For each strategy:

  - freeze the first date on which every required point-in-time input and
    warm-up period is available and the strategy can causally emit risk-on or
    risk-off;
  - give both the strategy and matched B&H the same $1,000 on that date;
  - use the same first obtainable next-open timing, costs, distribution
    convention, and unrounded arithmetic;
  - let B&H buy and hold TECL;
  - let the strategy follow its actual signal, including earning zero while
    risk-off; and
  - treat inadequate remaining history as insufficient evidence rather than
    moving the start date.

Reasoning. This directly answers whether following the complete strategy was
better than buying and holding over the same opportunity period, without letting
the strategy choose a flattering comparison start through its first trade.

9. CONFIRM THE MODELED ORDER-SIZE RANGE
================================================================================

Context. Questionnaire 5 asked which normal order-size band the execution study
should model. Its suggested answer listed several alternatives but did not
recommend one specific band. The response "Your answer is good" therefore
accepted the principle of using a band without actually selecting one. The active
plan later introduced $10,000-$100,000 as if it had been explicitly selected.

Plain version. Montauk does not need to know Max's account balance or actual
order size. It does need one frozen scale for testing whether the next-open
slippage and cost assumptions remain conservative at plausible retail order
sizes.

Question. Should Montauk use $10,000-$100,000 as its fixed modeled
position-change order range?

Suggested answer. Yes. Model every Gold backtest and execution study across the
$10,000-$100,000 notional range and use the conservative result where estimated
costs differ materially within it. This is only the range over which Montauk
makes its modeled execution claim. It is not an account-size disclosure, an
instruction to trade that amount, or permission to collect, infer, enforce, or
reconcile actual balances, positions, orders, or fills.

If this range does not safely cover the largest normal TECL position-change
order the model should contemplate, state the preferred upper band. If orders
materially below $10,000 also need their own cost treatment, state that here.

Reasoning. The plan cannot calibrate a reproducible execution claim against a
range that was never actually selected by the owner.

10. SHOULD 3.0 USE SLACK OR REQUIRE A SLACK-VERSUS-BUZZ BAKE-OFF?
================================================================================

Context. The active plan defines one provider-neutral conversation-channel
contract, uses Slack Socket Mode as the conservative default, and proposes a
time-boxed Slack-versus-Buzz bake-off before full channel implementation. It
currently says Max will make the final UX choice from that evidence. No
questionnaire has asked whether that comparison is worth making.

Plain version. The deterministic Montauk controller, permissions, commands, and
audit rules are the same either way. The choice affects only the private
conversation surface. Supporting and operating two live command paths is not
allowed.

Question. Should Montauk 3.0 commit to Slack now, or preserve the bake-off and a
later owner selection?

Suggested answer. Commit to one private custom Slack app using Socket Mode for
Montauk 3.0. Keep the internal adapter provider-neutral so Slack can be replaced
later without changing Montauk authority, but do not make a Buzz deployment or
bake-off a 3.0 build gate. Reconsider Buzz only through a later owner-directed
change if Slack demonstrates a concrete reliability, phone UX, continuity, or
operational deficiency.

Alternative. Preserve the time-boxed bake-off. If chosen, Montauk first builds
and tests the provider-neutral adapter, then presents a short evidence report on
phone/push reliability, thread continuity, identity and confirmation safety,
restart recovery, Debian resource cost, and operating burden. Max selects one
winner; the rejected adapter is not left running.

Reasoning. Fixing Slack now removes a nonessential comparison and a future owner
decision. Preserving the bake-off may produce a better conversation experience,
but deliberately retains one later decision before the channel is complete.

11. CONFIRM THE FINAL DECISION BOUNDARY
================================================================================

Context. "The last questionnaire" cannot mean Montauk may make future
owner-authority decisions autonomously. It means the intended product,
architecture, scope, and build behavior are sufficiently specified that no more
broad clarification questionnaire is needed. Some later choices can only be made
honestly after Phase 1 or commissioning produces evidence.

Question. Is the following the complete boundary between later owner approvals
and decisions the implementation team may make without asking another broad
questionnaire?

Suggested answer. Yes.

Later Max approval is still required only for:

  1. the final measured Phase 1 Gold/validation package, including the
     understandable false-Gold operating policy, confidence/evidence-sufficiency
     settings, provisional performance margin, activation/renewal thresholds,
     ranking ambiguity rules, and any empirically justified synthetic weight;
  2. any later proposal to add a third Montauk Score ingredient or turn a
     disclosed risk into a new Gold-disqualifying veto;
  3. reviewing the agent's capability-gap report and personally writing or
     directing any missing protected Rust building block, followed by the
     password-controlled reviewed-release sealing process;
  4. planning and beginning each non-authoritative clean-machine restore drill;
  5. each exact Active-strategy switch, including the one-time Montauk 2.0 to
     Montauk 3.0 signal-authority cutover;
  6. declaring Montauk 3.0 operationally complete or authorizing later-version
     work; and
  7. explicitly scheduling the optional Chimera study after its prerequisites
     exist.

If item 10 preserves the Slack-versus-Buzz bake-off, selecting its one production
winner is also a later Max approval. If item 10 fixes Slack, it is not.

Everything else that does not change those owner-visible boundaries is delegated
to evidence-driven implementation. This includes exact schemas, parser caps,
numeric tolerances derived from fixtures, queue and resource settings measured
on the actual host, database/index layouts, retry mechanics, test fixtures,
provider API details, command-envelope fields, idempotency construction,
cooperative cancellation, documentation corrections, and other reversible
engineering details.

The accepted implementation baseline also remains: one dedicated native Debian
appliance supervised by systemd; private Tailscale/SSH administration; a
subscription-first model adapter with a documented API fallback; one
provider-neutral conversation gateway; free verified authoritative market data
only; manual brokerage execution; and GitHub as the current off-machine recovery
path. Exact hardware purchase, personal portfolio information, provider spending
budgets, and throughput targets remain outside the Gold definition and are
commissioning concerns rather than new product questionnaires.

Escalation rule. An implementation agent asks one focused question—not a new
questionnaire—only if an unforeseen issue would materially change owner-visible
behavior, the Gold promise, protected-core authority, real-money safety,
external spending, credentials, or the explicit scope above. Otherwise it makes
the smallest defensible reversible engineering choice, records it, and proceeds.

Reasoning. This preserves Max's actual authority while preventing ordinary
technical choices, calibration work, or documentation defects from causing
another round of broad planning questions.
